Prottay Kumar Adhikary - Doctoral Researcher at IIT Delhi

P K A

In 2024, Prottay started his Ph.D. in the Computer Technology group of the Department of Electrical Engineering at IIT Delhi. He had already spent a short stint at the LCS2 lab the year before as a research assistant, working on counselling conversations, and his Ph.D. picks up where that work left off.

His research asks a simple question: how can large language models genuinely help in mental health care? He works on understanding and summarising therapy conversations, on judging model outputs by what clinicians actually need, and on small models that behave the way a good counsellor would. Much of this happens alongside psychiatrists and psychologists, who help shape both the questions and the data.

His coursework reflects the same mix. Alongside Deep Learning for NLP, Introduction to Large Language Models, Computational Neuroscience, Social Network Analysis, Cloud Computing and Data Visualization, he took Psychological Interventions; Ethnography: Theory, Practice and Critique; and Human Values and Technology, courses that sit closer to psychology and the social sciences than to computer science.


In 2019, Prottay came to India to study Computer Science and Engineering at NIT Silchar, and graduated in 2023 with a CGPA of 8.32/10. The first year was a broad one: mathematics, physics, chemistry, basic electrical and electronics engineering, engineering mechanics, environmental science, communicative English, and his first formal course in programming.

From the second year on, the degree turned into computer science proper. Data structures, discrete structures, algorithms and the theory of computation came first, followed by computer architecture, microprocessors, operating systems, networks, databases and software engineering. In the later semesters he studied graph theory, programming language principles, compiler design, graphics and multimedia, blockchain, and natural language processing, the field his research would grow out of.

Research started early. In the summer of 2021 he interned at IIT Guwahati, building a model to detect hate speech in code-mixed Assamese and Bengali YouTube comments. Back at NIT Silchar, he spent the following winter on how English-Assamese machine translation handles negation, and the summer of 2022 on translating lecture videos with lip-synced speech. That last project became his undergraduate thesis, TRAVID, and several of these projects turned into his first publications.
